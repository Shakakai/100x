This one started with a genealogy rabbit hole. A great-great-grandfather, a parish record, and a name on a shipping manifest pointed at County Leitrim — not the Ireland of tour buses, but a quiet green corner in the northwest that most people drive through on the way to somewhere else. So we went.

## The Ballroom of Romance

Glenfarne's claim to history is a ruined **ballroom by a lake** — the Ballroom of Romance, immortalized in a William Trevor story and a film most people haven't seen. It stands there half-collapsed among the trees, and standing in it feels like reading someone's diary. The lake is still. The midges are not.

## The Research

The actual genealogy work was three mornings in a parish office with a patient volunteer named Margaret, who produced baptismal records like a magician and gently corrected my pronunciation of every townland name. The results were **inconclusive in the satisfying way** — a few new names, a few new questions, and a graveyard we never quite located.

> The genealogy was inconclusive. The Guinness was not.

## The Sessions

The evenings belonged to the pubs. A session that started at 4pm and technically never ended. A fiddle player who turned out to be a distant something-or-other, because in Leitrim everyone is a distant something-or-other. Rain that arrived **horizontally** and was treated by the locals as a mild inconvenience.

I came home with a photocopied record, a hundred blurry photos of gravestones, and a standing invitation to come back. I'll take them up on it.
