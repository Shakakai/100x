Hanoi is not a city you ease into. You land, you step into the Old Quarter, and the scooter river immediately begins negotiating with your survival instincts. The technique, as everyone tells you and no one believes: **walk slowly, keep a steady pace, and believe**. The scooters do the rest. It works. It never stops feeling like it shouldn't.

## The Stool Circuit

The food program — and there is always a food program — was built around **tiny plastic stools**:

- **Phở gánh** from a dawn vendor, eaten on the sidewalk as the city switched on
- **Bún chả** at the place with the Obama combo, because of course
- **Bánh mì** from a cart with a line of locals, which is the only review system that matters
- **Egg coffee** — sounds like a prank, tastes like tiramisu in a cup, order two

## The City

Between meals: the Temple of Literature in the rain. The water puppet theater, which is stranger and better than it sounds. **Train Street**, where a freight train threads a corridor barely wider than itself while cafes pull their tables back six inches and then carry on — it lived up to the name.

> Hanoi runs on a frequency you can't hear at first. By day three you're part of the signal.

## Ha Long Bay

The side quest: two days on Ha Long Bay, kayaking between limestone karsts in fog so thick the islands appeared and disappeared like something being rendered on a slow connection. Slept on the boat. Woke up inside a painting.

Hanoi recalibrated my baseline for what a city can be. The stools were low. The bar was high.
