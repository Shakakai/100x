Ten days, one rental car, and roughly 1,300 kilometers of Route 1. The plan was loose: circle the island counterclockwise, sleep when the car stopped, and let the weather make all the real decisions. Iceland in June never gets dark, which destroys your sense of time in the best possible way — you stop asking "what time is it" and start asking "is the light good," and the answer is always yes.

## The South Coast

The first two days were a greatest-hits reel. **Seljalandsfoss** at 2am, completely alone, walking behind the curtain of water in full daylight — one of those moments that feels staged for you personally. Skógafoss in the rain. The black sand at **Reynisfjara**, where the basalt columns look like a pipe organ and the sneaker waves have genuinely killed people who got too casual.

> Icelandic weather doesn't change day to day. It changes windshield to windshield.

## The East and the North

The East Fjords are where the tour buses give up. Long, empty roads threading between mountains and cold grey water, fishing villages with one gas station and one excellent bakery. We detoured to **Jökulsárlón**, the glacier lagoon, and stood there redefining the color blue while seals worked the ice like it was a conveyor belt.

Mývatn smelled like sulfur and looked like Mars. Dettifoss sounded like the end of the world.

## Lessons from the Ring

- Gas station hot dogs **earned the hype**. Pylsur are a food group.
- Every "quick stop" costs 45 minutes because the light is always doing something.
- The wind opens car doors for you, violently. Hold the handle.
- Sheep have right of way, and they know it.

Would I do it again? I'm already pricing campervans.
