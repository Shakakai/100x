Belize was the pandemic-era trip — planned twice, canceled once, finally taken in a window of negative tests and open borders. It split cleanly in two: the **reef** and the **jungle**, and both delivered.

## The Reef

The **Belize Barrier Reef** is the second largest in the world, and snorkeling it feels like hovering over a city — coral towers, schools of fish moving like weather systems, a nurse shark that ignored us with professional indifference. At Hol Chan we drifted the cut with the current, fins barely moving, watching rays cruise the sand flats below.

## The Jungle

Inland, near Belmopan and San Ignacio, the trip changed registers entirely:

- **Xunantunich** — climbed El Castillo and looked out over Guatemala from a Mayan temple summit
- **ATM Cave** — waded, swam, and climbed through a cave system that was a Mayan ceremonial site, with calcified pottery still sitting where it was left a thousand years ago. No photos allowed, which made it the most memorable day of the trip
- A jungle lodge where the **howler monkeys** handled wakeup calls. They sound like a diesel engine possessed, and it is an extremely effective alarm clock

> The reef shows off. The jungle keeps secrets. Belize does both on the same day.

Ten days, two ecosystems, zero regrets. The rum punch situation on Caye Caulker is also worth investigating.
