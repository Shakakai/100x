The Big Island is five climate zones and a live volcano wearing a lei. A week based in Kona: coffee country on the slopes of Hualālai by day, and one of the strangest and best wildlife encounters of my life after dark.

## The Coffee Belt

Kona coffee grows on a narrow band of volcanic slope between roughly 700 and 2,500 feet, and the farms are small, family operations clinging to hillsides with million-dollar views. The farm tour circuit:

- A fourth-generation farm where the owner explained pruning with the intensity of a surgeon
- A **cupping session** that revealed my palate to be, charitably, "developing"
- Learning that "Kona blend" is 10% Kona and 90% disappointment — buy single-estate or don't bother

The smell of a roaster running in an open-air shed above the Pacific is now permanently filed under "good days."

## The Mantas

The headline event: **night snorkeling with manta rays**. You float on the surface holding a light board, the plankton come to the light, and then the mantas come to the plankton — twelve-foot wingspans doing slow barrel rolls inches below your chest. It is silent, weightless, and utterly unreal. One of them looped beneath me close enough that I could have counted gill slits.

> Nothing on land prepares you for a manta ray doing ballet under your ribs at 9pm.

## The Volcano

The Big Island finale was **Volcanoes National Park** — the Kīlauea caldera steaming in the rain, a hike across a crater floor that erupted sixty years ago, and the quiet understanding that the ground here is a suggestion, not a fact.

Flew home with four bags of coffee, a minor wetsuit tan, and a new benchmark for "night swim."
