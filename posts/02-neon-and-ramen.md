Tokyo had been on the list for a decade, and it still managed to be better than the version I'd built up in my head. A week split between the neon canyons of **Shinjuku**, the vintage shops of **Shimokitazawa**, and long, aimless walks that always seemed to end at a counter with eight seats and a vending machine for a maître d'.

## The Food Program

The trip had no itinerary, but it absolutely had a **food program**:

- **Ramen** — five shops in seven days, including a tsukemen place where the noodles arrive cold and the broth arrives volcanic
- **Standing sushi** at 7am after the Tsukiji outer market, still the best meal of the trip
- **Konbini runs** — 7-Eleven egg salad sandwiches are a legitimate culinary achievement and I will die on this hill
- An izakaya under the train tracks in Yurakucho where the smoke, the skewers, and the highballs all blur together

## Getting Lost on Purpose

The best afternoons were the unplanned ones. Golden Gai's six-seat bars. A retro game shop in Akihabara where I touched a mint-condition Neo Geo and understood collector psychology for the first time. The jazz kissaten where talking above a whisper feels illegal.

> Tokyo runs like a watch and reads like a novel. Every neighborhood is a different genre.

## Notes for Next Time

Book the teamLab tickets in advance. Bring an extra suitcase. Learn fifty more words of Japanese, because the ten I had did more work than any phrasebook ever has.
